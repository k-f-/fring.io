AI Weekly Brief — week ending 2026-11-07

This Week in AI

  • Voters put AI on notice. Democrats swept the midterms, top Democrats called for a national data center moratorium, and the White House canceled a planned tech panel. AI stocks sold off, with Nvidia falling 9% in its worst day since the DeepSeek shock. The New York Times, CNBC, The Economic Times
  • Moonshot AI published Kimi K3.1 Pro weights. The 2.8-trillion-parameter model ships at about 1.56TB on Hugging Face, and new spending data puts Kimi at 61% of US enterprise open-model spend. XenoSpectrum, South China Morning Post
  • OpenAI announced GPT-6 Cyber. It is a cybersecurity model for finding exploitable vulnerabilities and developing fixes, trained to stay within authorized scope. OpenAI
  • Google cut Gemini video prices 30%. Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash adds native 15-second video generation and editing with stronger consistency. Google
  • Copilot Chat has caps Microsoft will not publish. Enterprise users keep discovering undisclosed usage limits by hitting them, a planning risk for any organization that standardized on the tool. Computerworld, The Register
  • Claude in Microsoft Copilot joins the EU Data Boundary. Microsoft is bringing Anthropic's Claude models under its EU data residency commitment, which matters for any regulated deployment. Microsoft
  • Databricks set a runtime deadline for AI Functions. SQL AI Functions will require Databricks Runtime 17.3 LTS or above starting January 2027, and Apache Spark 4.3 landed in Runtime 19 this week. Databricks, Databricks
  • CMS finalized two major rules. The agency set a permanent Medicare drug price negotiation framework with prices effective in 2029, and finalized the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule. CMS, CMS

Regulatory Radar

US Federal

  • Democrats plan a wave of AI bills when they take control of Congress, and coverage frames the midterms as the start of an AI scrutiny era. Expect more federal proposals on automated decision-making and data use that reach health plans. Reuters, The New York Times
  • The Justice Department created a task force to police AI misuse. Federal enforcement attention on AI-assisted fraud can reach claims and payment workflows. Government Executive, Nextgov/FCW
  • The White House canceled a high-profile tech panel after the midterms, while top Democrats called for a national data center moratorium. The federal climate for AI infrastructure buildout hardened this week. Reuters, CNBC
  • The EPA inspector general will audit the agency's AI use. Watchdog audits of government AI are a template other inspectors general, including HHS OIG, can reuse. Chemical & Engineering News

US States

  • Democrats took control of state legislatures, including the Texas House, in sweeping state-level gains. State AI bills now move through more Democratic chambers, which raises the odds of new disclosure and automated-decision rules that bind insurers. The Washington Post, USA Today

Healthcare-specific

  • CMS finalized the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program rule (CMS-4215-F), a permanent negotiation framework starting with prices effective in 2029. Part D plans get a more predictable negotiation calendar. CMS press release, CMS fact sheet
  • CMS finalized the CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule. Payment rule changes flow into claims pricing and encounter edits for 2027 dates of service. CMS
  • CMS proposed the CARE rule (CMS-3484-P) to cut administrative requirements in Medicare and Medicaid. Fewer provider requirements can change what documentation arrives with claims and encounters. CMS
